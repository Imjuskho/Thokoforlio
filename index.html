About

Evidence is only as powerful as the hands it reaches.

Information withheld is power withheld. When health knowledge stays inside journals and donor reports, communities cannot choose for themselves.

Thokozani Phanga is a health communications specialist and founder of Zisamale Health in Lilongwe, Malawi. Her work centres on knowledge translation and research dissemination, with a research focus on health information intermediaries and unequal access to health knowledge.

She has coordinated health and development programmes for NGOs and international donors and now delivers programme management and health campaign design and implementation through Zisamale Health.

She has a Bachelor’s degree in Social Science from the University of Malawi, Chancellor College, and is currently a Mastercard Foundation Scholar pursuing a PGDip in Global Health Research at the University of Oxford.

Oxford research inquiry

Health information intermediaries and unequal access to knowledge

World-class health research is produced in the communities that carry the heaviest burden of disease. Those communities rarely receive the findings. My scholarship asks how trusted local communicators can close that gap — so people can decide, and hold systems to account.

Translating health evidence into plain language is not communications support. It is health justice.

Selected work

Three pieces that show the method.

Strategic communications · Beit CURE Children's Hospital

Beit CURE Malawi launches cleft service line

I wrote an article announcing a new cleft lip and palate service at Beit CURE Children's Hospital. I turned clinical information into a clear story for families and partners, using patient stories and clinicians' voices, and ending with a direct call to action for families. The service line has since treated over 30 children with cleft lip and palate, in addition to the 2,500 orthopaedic surgeries the hospital performs each year.

Annual report · Publication

2025 CURE Children’s Hospital of Malawi Annual Report

I led the editorial development of the hospital's 2025 annual report. I wrote the stories, edited the Executive Director's letter and selected the images for each page. I coordinated with CURE's global marketing team to align the hospital's content with the wider organisation, keeping one consistent voice across the report. Published in March 2026 and distributed to donors and partners globally.

Research report · British Council

Fashion Futures: Namibia

I wrote a research report exploring youth engagement in sustainability practices within the fashion industry, drawing on qualitative research I conducted in Namibia. I interviewed designers, content creators, models and industry leaders in Windhoek, and translated what they told me into an editorial piece for a public audience, asking how young creatives can engage with sustainability in a growing fashion scene and what stands in their way. Published on Nataal in November 2019 as part of the Fashion Futures series with the British Council. The report was presented at the Fashion Futures Launch event.
